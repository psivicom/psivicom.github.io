01 — The Atomic Unit of Open Science

Scientific Identity
ORCID: The Permanent Identifier
In traditional academia, institutions own your identity. In open science, you own your identity. An ORCID iD is a free, permanent, 16-digit identifier that follows you across every repository, journal, and platform for the rest of your life. It is the foundational requirement for FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) data principles.

Why Independent Researchers Need It

Without an ORCID, automated systems (like Zenodo and GitHub) cannot reliably attribute your code and datasets to you. An ORCID bridges the gap between independent work and the formal academic record, ensuring your discoveries are permanently linked to your name.

How to Obtain It (5 Minutes)

  1. Visit orcid.org/register
  2. Use a stable, permanent email address (never a temporary or institutional email you might lose).
  3. Set your affiliation to "Independent Researcher" or your organization.
  4. Save your 16-digit iD (e.g., 0000-000X-XXXX-XXXX).

02 — The Zenodo Blueprint (Zero-Cost DOIs)

Zenodo is a free, open-access repository operated by CERN. It assigns permanent Digital Object Identifiers (DOIs) to your code and data. However, the order of operations is critical to avoid breaking your citations.

Step 1: The Sandbox

Always begin on sandbox.zenodo.org. This is CERN's testing environment. Log in with your ORCID. Generate an API token with deposit:actions and deposit:write scopes.

Step 2: Reserve the DOI

Click "New Upload" and immediately click "Reserve DOI". This locks in a permanent DOI (e.g., 10.5072/zenodo.123456) before you publish. You can now test your automated scripts against this exact DOI without creating duplicate records.

Step 3: Automate & Publish

Add the Sandbox API token and Reserved DOI to your GitHub Secrets. Run your zenodo_agent.py script. Once the metadata and files are verified on Sandbox, repeat the exact process on production zenodo.org.

03 — The Pitfalls (What to Avoid)

Many independent researchers lose their citations or lock themselves out of their own data. Here are the exact traps this blueprint avoids:

Pitfall #1: Publishing Before Testing
The Trap: You push to production Zenodo, realize the metadata is wrong, and publish it. Zenodo DOIs are permanent and immutable once published. You cannot delete or change a published DOI.
The Fix: Always use the Sandbox. Always use "Reserve DOI" so you can edit the metadata 50 times before clicking "Publish".
Pitfall #2: The Email Trap
The Trap: You register your ORCID with a university or work email. You change jobs or graduate, lose access to the email, and can no longer recover your ORCID account.
The Fix: Use a permanent personal email (Gmail, ProtonMail) or a custom domain email that you control forever. Add your institutional emails only as secondary aliases.
Pitfall #3: The Versioning Fragmentation
The Trap: You update your code and run your Zenodo script again. Zenodo creates a brand new DOI for the update. Now your software has 5 different DOIs, and citations are fractured.
The Fix: Use Zenodo's "New Version" feature for updates, which keeps the concept DOI (the master DOI) the same while assigning a specific DOI to the exact version. Update the version: "1.0.1" in your CITATION.cff file before every run.

04 — The Code: CITATION.cff

GitHub natively reads a file named CITATION.cff (Citation File Format) in your repository root. When this file exists, GitHub automatically generates a "Cite this repository" button on your repo's main page, and Zenodo reads it to auto-fill your metadata.

cff-version: 1.2.0
message: "If you use this software, please cite it using the metadata from this file."
title: "Mesh Pico AI: A Neuroplastic Intelligence Framework"
authors:
  - family-names: "Audette"
    given-names: "Louis-Philippe"
    orcid: "https://orcid.org/0000-000X-XXXX-XXXX"  # <-- Your actual ORCID
    affiliation: "PSIVI Research"
version: "1.0.0"
date-released: "2026-09-18"
url: "https://psivicom.github.io"
repository-code: "https://github.com/psivicom/psivicom.github.io"
license: "EUPL-1.2"
keywords:
  - "neuroplastic AI"
  - "decentralized computing"
  - "open science"
  - "FAIR principles"
type: software

Replace the ORCID and repository URLs in the CITATION.cff file, and you instantly have a FAIR-compliant, automated DOI pipeline for your own research.
